Pick Your Lane

6 questions that tell you more than any career test

The second post in the Pick Your Lane series.
By Dee

In the last post we talked about clues. That one thing that pulls you in without effort. The thing that makes you forget to check your phone.

But maybe you read that and thought — "okay, but I still don't know what my clue is."

Fair enough. So let's find it.

Not with a fancy career test that spits out "you should be an accountant" after 40 confusing questions. Just six honest questions. No right answers. No scores.

Find your clues

Read each question slowly. Then write whatever comes to mind, right there in the box. When you're done, I'll tell you what I noticed.

Question 1
What makes you forget to check your phone?

Not what sounds impressive. Not what your parents want to hear. The real answer.

Maybe you lose track of time when you're drawing. Or mid-game, working out the next move. Or explaining something to a friend until an hour has gone by.

That thing — whatever it is — isn't a distraction. It's a signal. Even if it sounds silly. Especially if it sounds silly.

Question 2
What do you do when you have nothing to do?

No homework. No plans. No one telling you what's next. Just you and a free afternoon.

Do you read? Reorganise your room for no reason? Call a friend and talk for two hours? Kick a ball? Fall into a YouTube rabbit hole about space or engines? Draw something random?

That unguarded moment — when nobody's watching and nothing's expected — is the most honest version of you.

Question 3
What kind of problems do you like solving?

And if your last answer was "I just watch stuff" — that's okay. But what are you watching? Cooking, football breakdowns, tech reviews, true crime, DIY builds? Even that is a clue.

Now — what kind of problem pulls you in? A puzzle you can't put down? A fight between friends you end up mediating? A broken gadget you have to open up? A story that nags until you figure out the ending?

Logical, human, mechanical, creative. The ones that don't feel like work — those are yours.

Question 4
What do people come to you for?

The other questions looked inward. This one looks outward — at how people already see you.

When friends have a problem, what do they bring you? "Help me figure this out"? "I just need to talk"? "Can you make this look better"? "Can you explain this"?

Or maybe no one comes to you yet — that's fine, you're 13. But even at home: are you the fixer, the listener, the explainer, the organiser, the entertainer? You already have a reputation. That's a clue.

Question 5
What would you do if marks didn't matter?

The dangerous question — because the honest answer might surprise you.

Imagine no report card. No ranks. No comparison with the cousin who got 95. Just you and whatever you'd spend your time on.

Cooking all day? Writing stories nobody asked for? Building with your hands? Starting a small business? Coding a game? Music for hours?

Notice what came in the first three seconds — not the "but that's not a real career" thought right after. Write the first one down before it disappears.

Question 6
What angers you about the world?

Last one, and it goes deep. Adults tell you to calm down, be positive, don't complain. But here's the secret — the things that anger you are the things you care about most.

Stray animals suffering while everyone walks past? Someone treated unfairly while people just watch? Things disorganised and nobody fixing them? Kids without good schools? Pollution, fake news, food wastage?

That anger is a value. The doctor who couldn't stand watching people suffer. The journalist who couldn't stand lies. The founder who couldn't stand a problem nobody was solving. They all started here. What's yours?

One more thing worth knowing — some of the careers your parents talk about may look very different by the time you're 25. AI is changing nearly everything. But here's what won't change: the human behind the work. Your curiosity, your way of seeing problems, the things you can't stop caring about. Those will matter more, not less.

Your answers stay private. Nothing is saved with your name.
